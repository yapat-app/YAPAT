Research Context

YAPAT's label-space workflow relates to two papers by researchers at DFKI and the University of Oldenburg. This page summarises what each paper reports and states which of its ideas the current software implements. The papers describe research directions and a demonstration system; they are not a specification of YAPAT.

Grounded Label Space Engineering for Knowledge-Centric Annotation Workflows

P. Sitapara, T. S. Gouvêa, and D. Sonntag. A challenge paper.

The paper starts from the observation that annotation tools usually expect the label set to be fixed before labelling begins, and that such label sets tend to be flat lists with no links to shared knowledge resources. It argues that experts often refine concepts or meet new phenomena while annotating. When the label set cannot follow, that knowledge is either squeezed into ill-fitting labels or kept in free-text comments that software cannot use. The authors call this loss of knowledge epistemic loss.

As a response, the paper proposes Grounded Label Space Engineering (GLSE) as a problem formulation and research direction. In GLSE, a label space is treated as a semantic object linked to ontologies, one that experts can build up, ground, revise, and stabilise, so that annotation and knowledge engineering develop together under governance rules. The paper names three operations: introducing new concepts during annotation, aligning concepts with existing resources where possible, and stabilising concepts through validation and reuse. Language models may assist with candidate retrieval or suggestions, but the paper keeps final authority with the human expert.

As a challenge paper, it presents a proof-of-concept in bioacoustic monitoring only briefly and reports no evaluation. It lists open problems, including incorrect language-model suggestions, fragmentation of concepts across annotators, and the effort that grounding places on experts who are not trained in ontology engineering, and it proposes evaluating GLSE in future work at the workflow, knowledge, and downstream levels.

Structuring Annotation Label Spaces by Natural Language Concept Elicitation and Ontology Grounding

P. Sitapara, P. Doddanawar, T. S. Gouvêa, and D. Sonntag. A demonstration paper for the GenAIK-NORA 2026 workshop (Joint Workshop on Generative AI and Knowledge Graphs and Knowledge Graphs & Agentic Systems Interplay), co-located with IJCAI-ECAI 2026, 17 August 2026, Bremen.

This paper describes a demonstration system in which an expert names the concepts to annotate in plain language before annotation starts. A language model, reached through an MCP server, calls tools that look up candidate concepts. Species names are looked up in GBIF and environmental terms in ENVO and OLS, with Wikipedia as a lower-priority source. Candidates are shown to the expert, and only confirmed ones join the label space. If no resource has a suitable entry, the system builds a local OWL ontology module for the concept and records where it came from. The confirmed concepts form a session ontology that is frozen before annotation and then constrains the labels. The paper works through a bird-annotation example and lists its own limitations: it is a demonstration rather than an empirical study, the local concepts are not yet aligned with external ontologies, and the set of supported ontologies is small. The paper links to a demonstration video at cst.dfki.de/projects-grounded-label-space-demo and names the oe_yapat repository as its code.

What the software implements

The table compares ideas from the papers with the public repositories. The right-hand column describes the code, not the papers.

Idea in the papers In the current repositories
Naming concepts in natural language Implemented as the label-space chat, which forwards prompts to the oe_yapat service.
Tool-based lookup in GBIF, ENVO/OLS, and Wikipedia Implemented as MCP tools in oe_yapat.
Expert confirmation before a concept enters the label space Implemented: returned candidates are added to the label space by the user, all at once or one by one, and can be removed again before submission.
Local OWL modules for concepts without an external match oe_yapat can write such a module as a Turtle file. The YAPAT backend keeps only the concept entry the service returns.
Explicit grounding-status categories Not found in the backend or frontend. Each entry keeps the identifier and the source details the service returned.
Provenance of label-space construction Partly: the backend stores the chat history and records who added each concept and when.
A frozen session ontology exported as OWL or JSON-LD Not found. Label-space versions are stored as JSON in the database.
Governance of the label space as it evolves YAPAT has a submit-and-promote step: a member submits a version and a team owner activates one. The papers describe governance in general terms and do not specify this mechanism.
Evaluation at workflow, knowledge, and downstream levels Proposed in the papers as future work; the repositories contain no such evaluation.
Related pages. What is a Label Space?, Grounding, and Managing versions describe the implemented workflow.
