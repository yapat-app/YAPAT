Teams

Teams group users with the datasets and label spaces they share. In the user-study version, team administration happens on the team list, which administrators use; the team detail page does not load (see below).

Roles

Every account has one global role, shown on the Dashboard as Administrator, Team owner, or Annotator. Team membership has its own role, owner or user.

Global role What the interface offers
Admin Sees all datasets and teams. Registers datasets, generates embeddings, creates and deletes teams, and creates team invitation links. The only role that sees the Study Logs page.
Team owner (team_owner) Sees the Team Management card. A team owner who already belongs to a team is sent straight to that team's detail page, which does not load in this version. Can change a dataset's spectrogram display settings.
User (Annotator) Annotates the datasets their teams can access. Does not see Team Management or Study Logs.

The team list

The Team Management card on the Dashboard is shown to administrators and team owners and opens the team list (/teams). There, administrators create teams, create invitation links with Invite People, and delete teams.

Creating a team

The Create Team dialog needs a team name and at least one dataset; its Create button stays disabled until both are given. A dataset can belong to only one team, so datasets already assigned elsewhere appear disabled. A team owner who creates a team becomes its owner; an administrator who creates a team does not.

The team detail page

Does not load in this version. The detail page (/teams/{team_id}) first requests GET /api/teams/{team_id}, and the user-study backend has no such route; it defines only DELETE on that path. Until the request succeeds the page shows "Team not found.", so the member list, invitation links, team editing, member removal, and team deletion offered on that page are not usable. Editing a team and removing a member would also call routes the backend does not have. This follows from the code; it was not observed in a running instance.

Invitations

The working invitation flow runs from the team list:

  1. Create the link. An administrator clicks Invite People for a team. The link is an owner invitation when the team has no owner yet, and a member invitation otherwise. Links expire after two days and have no limit on the number of uses.
  2. Open the link. It points to the sign-in page with the token and the intended role (/login/?token=…&target_role=owner or user). With a token present, the form registers a new account instead of signing in.
  3. Register. The new account joins the team as owner or user. The global role follows the link too: an owner link creates an account with the global role team_owner, and a member link one with the role user.

Because team owners with a team are redirected to the detail page, they cannot create invitation links in the user-study interface. There is also no control for revoking a link. The sign-up page creates an account with the global role user that belongs to no team.

Deleting a team

Administrators delete teams from the team list with the delete icon, which acts immediately without a confirmation step. The backend also accepts deletion by the team's owner, but the owner's delete control is on the detail page. Deleting a team unassigns its datasets — they are not deleted — and removes its memberships and invitations. Users who had access through the team lose it. This action is irreversible.

Access checks. The backend verifies team membership and dataset access on the operations that need them; administrators bypass team-level checks. See REST API for the endpoint-level rules.
